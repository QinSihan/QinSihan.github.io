PhysGenPARSIRI-Bench

Learning Physics from Pretrained Video Models: A Multimodal Continuous and Sequential World Interaction Models for Robotic Manipulation

Zijian Song, Qichang Li, Sihan Qin, Yuhao Chen, Tianshui Chen, Liang Lin, Guangrun Wang

ICMR 2026

arXivPDF

PhysGen explores how dynamics learned through video pretraining can support robot manipulation. A single model predicts future frames and actions, then uses new observations after execution to guide subsequent predictions.

Connecting video and action

Visual observations and action chunks are represented as continuous “physical tokens” and modeled autoregressively. Predicted tokens are decoded into future frames and executable actions; new observations feed into the next prediction. Causal masking, Lookahead Multi-Token Prediction (L-MTP), and KV caching address temporal dependencies and computational efficiency.

Evaluation setup

The paper evaluates the method on LIBERO, ManiSkill, and the four real-robot tasks shown above. Video pretraining is followed by fine-tuning on demonstrations for the target manipulation tasks, testing whether this pretraining can support downstream robot control.

Physical Autoregressive Model for Robotic Manipulation without Action Pretraining

Zijian Song, Sihan Qin, Tianshui Chen, Liang Lin, Guangrun Wang

arXiv · 2025

arXivPDFProject page & demos

PAR transfers dynamics learned through video pretraining to robot control by jointly generating future frames and actions. “Without action pretraining” refers to the absence of large-scale robot action pretraining; demonstrations are still needed to fine-tune the model on target tasks.

Approach

A causal autoregressive model predicts a shared sequence of frame and action tokens. Diffusion-based decoders generate the corresponding frames and actions. Observations collected after execution become context for later predictions, providing continuing feedback from the environment.

Evaluation setup

The paper evaluates PushCube, PickCube, and StackCube in ManiSkill, with ablations of the autoregressive structure and decoding method. The project page compares predicted frames with actual simulated execution.

My contribution

I contributed to model development, training and evaluation, and paper writing. The broader project also involved real-robot experiments and demonstration collection; see my HCP Lab experience for details.

SIRI-Bench: Challenging VLMs' Spatial Intelligence through Complex Reasoning Tasks

Zijian Song, Xiaoxin Lin, Qiuming Huang, Sihan Qin, Guangrun Wang, Liang Lin

arXiv · 2025

arXivPDF

SIRI-Bench places geometry problems in rendered 3D scenes to evaluate vision-language models (VLMs). Solving a problem requires finding relevant spatial relations in video and then reasoning from those conditions.

Constructing the problems

An automatic scene creation engine converts geometry problems into 3D scenes and videos. Key geometric conditions are conveyed visually, while auxiliary conditions and the question remain in text, requiring the model to use the visual input.

Evaluating perception and reasoning

The experiments compare extracting conditions from video with reading complete conditions in text, and analyze errors in spatial perception and reasoning. This comparison helps distinguish missed or misread scene information from reasoning failures after the conditions are available.
